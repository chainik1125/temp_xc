\title{Four steering conventions for temporal dictionaries\\
{\large what each one actually writes into the residual stream}}
\author{}
\date{2026-07-27}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Four procedures in this codebase are all called ``steering a temporal dictionary latent'',
and they write different things. Two of them --- the paper's \texttt{v7} tiled-broadcast hook
and this sprint's \texttt{txc\_flat} control --- turn out to be the \emph{same} intervention up
to a scalar, even though one is the published method and the other was built to be a null. A
third, the paper's \texttt{pp} per-position hook, collapses onto the same write at interior
positions whenever the clamped feature's activation is roughly constant across windows. Only
the fourth, \texttt{txc\_slab}, applies a temporally structured write to the residual stream.
This note derives each claim explicitly. The organising fact is a decomposition: a decoder slab
splits into a position-independent (DC) part and a mean-zero (AC) part, the DC part is exactly
what a per-token dictionary can already express, and three of the four procedures write only
the DC part.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and notation}

Fix a transformer, a hook layer $\ell$, and a prompt of $S$ tokens. Write
$h \in \Rbb^{S \times d}$ for the residual stream at layer $\ell$, with $h_p \in \Rbb^d$ the
activation at token $p$. A \emph{steering intervention} replaces $h$ by $h + \Delta$ for some
$\Delta \in \Rbb^{S\times d}$. Everything below is a recipe for choosing $\Delta$.

Let $T$ be the window length and $h$ (overloaded, but standard) the dictionary width. The two
architectures differ in the shape of their decoder.

\paragraph{Per-token SAE.} The code has one vector per token, $z \in \Rbb^{S \times h}$, and the
decoder is $\Wdec \in \Rbb^{h\times d}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_p \;=\; \sum_{j} z_{p,j}\,\Wdec[j,:] \;+\; b_{\mathrm{dec}} .
\end{equation}
Latent $j$ owns a single \emph{direction} $\Wdec[j,:] \in \Rbb^d$.

\paragraph{Temporal crosscoder (TXC).} The code is \emph{shared across the window},
$z \in \Rbb^{h}$ per window, and the decoder carries a position axis,
$\Wdec \in \Rbb^{h\times T\times d}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t \;=\; \sum_j z_j \, \Wdec[j,t,:] \;+\; b_{\mathrm{dec}},
  \qquad t = 0,\dots,T-1 .
  \label{eq:txc-decode}
\end{equation}
Latent $j$ owns an entire matrix. We call it the \emph{slab}:
\begin{equation}
  \slab^{(j)} \;:=\; \Wdec[j] \;\in\; \Rbb^{T\times d},
  \qquad
  \slab^{(j)}_t \;=\; \Wdec[j,t,:] \in \Rbb^{d}.
\end{equation}
The row $\slab^{(j)}_t$ is the direction the latent writes at position $t$. Because the rows are
free to differ, $\operatorname{rank}\slab^{(j)}$ can exceed $1$; that is the entire structural
difference between the two architectures.

\begin{definition}[DC/AC decomposition]
\label{def:dcac}
For a slab $\slab \in \Rbb^{T\times d}$ define the \emph{position mean}
$\dc := \tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\slab_t \in \Rbb^d$ and split
\begin{equation}
  \slab \;=\; \underbrace{\one_T \otimes \dc}_{\text{DC part}} \;+\; \ac,
  \qquad
  \ac_t := \slab_t - \dc,
  \qquad
  \sum_{t}\ac_t = 0 .
  \label{eq:dcac}
\end{equation}
The DC part is rank $1$ and constant in $t$. The AC part is mean-zero across positions and
carries all of the slab's temporal structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The DC part is precisely what a per-token dictionary can produce: writing a fixed direction
$v$ at every position is $\one_T\otimes v$. So $\ac$ is the \emph{only} component of a slab
that a per-token dictionary cannot express at any steering strength. Any procedure that
discards $\ac$ cannot, even in principle, exhibit an advantage for the crosscoder's extra
expressiveness.
\end{remark}

\section{The four procedures}

\subsection{V7 --- tiled broadcast (the paper's default)}
\label{sec:v7}

Source: \verb|_build_v7_hook| in \verb|src/temp_bench/case_studies/steering.py|; selected by
\verb|protocol="v7"|, which is the default in \verb|SteeringConfig| and in the
\verb|experiments/c5_steering/run.py| CLI.

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Tile the prefix into non-overlapping blocks of length $T$ starting at token $0$:
        block starts $b T$ for $b = 0,\dots,\lfloor S/T\rfloor - 1$. If $S \bmod T > 0$, append
        one further window aligned to the \emph{right} edge, starting at $S-T$.
  \item Encode each block to get its code $z^{(b)}$.
  \item \textbf{Clamp} coordinate $j$ to an absolute value $s$: $z^{(b),c} = z^{(b)} + (s - z^{(b)}_j)e_j$.
  \item Decode both codes and take the difference,
        $\delta^{(b)} := \hat{x}(z^{(b),c}) - \hat{x}(z^{(b)}) \in \Rbb^{T\times d}$.
  \item \textbf{Average over the window}, $\bar\delta^{(b)} := \tfrac1T\sum_t \delta^{(b)}_t \in \Rbb^d$.
  \item Write $\bar\delta^{(b)}$ \emph{identically} to all $T$ positions of block $b$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What it writes.} By \eqref{eq:txc-decode} the decoder is linear in $z$, so clamping a
single coordinate gives
\begin{equation}
  \delta^{(b)}_t \;=\; \bigl(s - z^{(b)}_j\bigr)\,\slab^{(j)}_t
  \;=\; c_b\, \slab^{(j)}_t,
  \qquad c_b := s - z^{(b)}_j \in \Rbb .
  \label{eq:clamp-delta}
\end{equation}
The clamp produces a scalar multiple of the slab --- nothing more. Averaging and broadcasting:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Delta^{\mathrm{V7}}_p \;=\; c_b\,\dc^{(j)}
  \quad\text{for every }p\text{ in block }b.\;}
  \label{eq:v7-write}
\end{equation}

\subsection{PP --- per-position (the paper's fallback)}
\label{sec:pp}

Source: \verb|_build_pp_hook|, selected by \verb|protocol="pp"|. Used only when a V7 pre-test
fails a coherence threshold.

\paragraph{Algorithm.} Identical to V7 through step 4, except the windows slide with stride $1$
($K = S-T+1$ of them), and instead of averaging over $t$ the per-position deltas are scattered
back to absolute positions and averaged over \emph{overlap}:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}_p \;=\; \frac{1}{n_p}\sum_{w\,:\,0\le p-w\le T-1,\;0\le w\le K-1}
    c_w\,\slab^{(j)}_{\,p-w},
  \qquad
  n_p := \#\{w\} .
  \label{eq:pp-write}
\end{equation}
Window $w$ covers absolute positions $w,\dots,w+T-1$, so its row $\tau$ lands at $p = w+\tau$;
equivalently row index $\tau = p-w$.

\subsection{\texttt{txc\_slab} --- the sprint's crosscoder arm}

Source: \verb|experiments/temporal_screen/txc_wins/harness.py|. The document is cut into $T$
content-aligned segments and the raw decoder slab is injected once, normalised to unit Frobenius
norm:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Delta^{\mathrm{slab}}_t \;=\; \alpha\,
  \frac{\slab^{(j)}_t}{\lVert \slab^{(j)}\rVert_F}\;}
  \label{eq:slab-write}
\end{equation}
Row $t$ is applied to segment $t$, exactly once, with no averaging.

\subsection{\texttt{txc\_flat} --- the sprint's profile-removal control}

Same latent, same total injected norm, temporal profile deleted. With
$\slab := \slab^{(j)}/\lVert\slab^{(j)}\rVert_F$ and $m := \tfrac1T\sum_t \slab_t$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Delta^{\mathrm{flat}}_t \;=\; \alpha\,\frac{m}{\sqrt{T}\,\lVert m\rVert_2}
  \quad\text{(independent of } t\text{)}.\;}
  \label{eq:flat-write}
\end{equation}
Its purpose in the sprint is adversarial: if steering with $\Delta^{\mathrm{flat}}$ matches
steering with $\Delta^{\mathrm{slab}}$, the temporal profile is doing nothing and no temporal
claim is available.

\section{Equivalences}

\begin{proposition}[V7 is the flat control]
\label{prop:v7-flat}
For a temporal crosscoder, $\Delta^{\mathrm{V7}}$ and $\Delta^{\mathrm{flat}}$ are the same
intervention up to a positive scalar. Specifically, both are constant in position and both point
along $\dc^{(j)}$; they differ only in magnitude, $c_b$ versus
$\alpha/(\sqrt{T}\lVert m\rVert_2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \eqref{eq:v7-write} and \eqref{eq:flat-write}: the V7 write is $c_b\dc^{(j)}$ and
the flat write is a positive multiple of $m = \dc^{(j)}/\lVert\slab^{(j)}\rVert_F$, which is a
positive multiple of $\dc^{(j)}$. Neither depends on $t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[PP is a convolution, and collapses to flat in the interior]
\label{prop:pp}
Let $p$ be an interior position, $T-1\le p\le K-1$, so that all $T$ windows containing $p$ exist
and $n_p = T$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}_p \;=\; \frac1T\sum_{\tau=0}^{T-1} c_{\,p-\tau}\,\slab^{(j)}_{\tau}
  \;=\; \frac1T\,\bigl(c \star \slab^{(j)}\bigr)_p ,
\end{equation}
a cross-correlation of the scalar sequence $(c_w)_w$ with the rows of the slab. Writing
$c_w = \bar c + \epsilon_w$ with $\sum$ of $\epsilon$ over the relevant window zero-mean,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}_p \;=\; \underbrace{\bar c\,\dc^{(j)}}_{\text{the V7 / flat write}}
  \;+\; \frac1T\sum_{\tau} \epsilon_{\,p-\tau}\,\ac^{(j)}_{\tau}.
  \label{eq:pp-decomp}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the clamped feature's activation is constant across windows ($\epsilon\equiv 0$),
then $\Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}_p = \bar c\,\dc^{(j)}$ exactly --- identical to V7.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For interior $p$, as $w$ runs over $p-T+1,\dots,p$ the row index $\tau = p-w$ runs over
$T-1,\dots,0$, hitting each row exactly once; substituting into \eqref{eq:pp-write} with
$n_p=T$ gives the first line. For the second, substitute $c_{p-\tau} = \bar c + \epsilon_{p-\tau}$
and use $\tfrac1T\sum_\tau \slab_\tau = \dc$ together with
$\slab_\tau = \dc + \ac_\tau$ and $\sum_\tau \ac_\tau = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
PP recovers temporal structure only through the \emph{variation of the clamped latent's
activation across windows}, and only at positions where that variation is appreciable relative to
its mean. At the sequence edges ($p<T-1$ or $p>K-1$) fewer than $T$ windows contribute, $n_p<T$,
and a partial profile survives for a boundary layer of width $T-1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Only the slab writes AC]
Using \eqref{eq:dcac},
\[
  \Delta^{\mathrm{slab}}_t \;=\; \frac{\alpha}{\lVert \slab^{(j)}\rVert_F}
  \Bigl(\dc^{(j)} + \ac^{(j)}_t\Bigr),
\]
whereas $\Delta^{\mathrm{V7}}$ and $\Delta^{\mathrm{flat}}$ are exactly
$(\text{scalar})\cdot\dc^{(j)}$ and contain no $\ac$ component, and $\Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}$
contains only the $\epsilon$-modulated image of $\ac$ in \eqref{eq:pp-decomp}.
\end{proposition}

\section{Why this is the whole question}

A per-token dictionary latent, steered arbitrarily, reaches writes of the form
$\one_T \otimes v$ --- one direction, possibly with a time-varying gain if the coefficient is
allowed to vary, hence any rank-$1$ matrix. The crosscoder's claim to extra power rests entirely
on $\ac$: the part of the slab that is \emph{not} expressible as a constant direction.

Consequently:
\begin{center}
\emph{A steering protocol that averages over the window before writing cannot demonstrate a
crosscoder advantage in expressiveness, because it deletes the only component in which such an
advantage could appear.}
\end{center}
This is not a claim that the protocol is wrong --- it is even-handed, since for a per-token SAE
($z\in\Rbb^{S\times h}$) V7 averages the per-position delta too. It is a claim about what the
resulting numbers can and cannot support.

\subsection{A worked micro-example}

Take $T=3$, $d=2$, and a latent whose slab writes $+e_1$ early, nothing in the middle, and
$-e_1$ late, plus a constant nudge along $e_2$:
\begin{equation}
  \slab \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 & \tfrac13\\[2pt]
    0 & \tfrac13\\[2pt]
   -1 & \tfrac13
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \dc = \Bigl(0,\ \tfrac13\Bigr),
  \qquad
  \ac = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0\\ 0&0\\ -1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The slab has rank $2$. Its temporally interesting content --- push one way at the start, the
other way at the end --- lives entirely in the first coordinate and is \emph{exactly mean-zero}.
Therefore:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta^{\mathrm{slab}}$ writes $+e_1$ at $t=0$ and $-e_1$ at $t=2$: the effect is present.
  \item $\Delta^{\mathrm{V7}} = \Delta^{\mathrm{flat}} \propto (0,\tfrac13)$ at every position:
        the $e_1$ structure has cancelled \emph{identically}. The intervention is blind to it.
  \item $\Delta^{\mathrm{PP}}$ at an interior $p$ equals
        $\tfrac13(c_p - c_{p-2})\,e_1 + \tfrac13\bar c\,e_2$, so it sees the $e_1$ structure only
        to the extent that the latent's activation differs between windows two apart.
\end{itemize}
An antisymmetric temporal profile --- arguably the canonical ``temporal'' feature, and the shape
of any ``first versus last'' or ``before versus after'' effect --- is in the exact kernel of the
tiled-broadcast protocol.

\section{Two axes of difference beyond the profile}

\paragraph{Position indexing: tokens versus content.}
V7 tiles from token $0$: block $b$ occupies absolute tokens $bT,\dots,bT+T-1$, so the row index
of a given piece of text is $p \bmod T$, which depends on the arbitrary phase of the tiling
relative to the prompt. Prepending a single token permutes every row assignment. PP inherits the
same token-indexing, softened by the overlap average. By contrast \texttt{txc\_slab} and
\texttt{txc\_flat} segment the \emph{document}, so ``position $t$'' means ``segment $t$ of this
example'' in every draw. Even a protocol that preserved $\ac$ would apply it at a random phase
under token tiling, which for content-aligned features averages it toward zero across examples.

\paragraph{Dose semantics: activation space versus residual space.}
V7 and PP fix the \emph{latent value} $z_j = s$ (``absolute clamp''), and let the decoder norm
determine how hard the residual stream is pushed; by \eqref{eq:clamp-delta} the injected norm is
$\lvert s - z_j\rvert\,\lVert\slab^{(j)}\rVert_F$, which varies per document and, critically,
across architectures whose decoder norms differ. \texttt{txc\_slab} and \texttt{txc\_flat} fix
the \emph{injected Frobenius norm} to $\alpha$ and let the implied latent value fall where it may.

Neither convention makes the comparison automatically fair. Matching on latent value leaves the
injected norm free; matching on injected norm leaves the per-position norm free, and a
concentrated write then places $\sqrt{T}$ times more norm on its dominant position than a
broadcast write does, so it leaves the linear regime at a lower nominal dose.\footnote{Measured
on the StruQ cell in this sprint: the gradient slab, whose mass sits almost entirely on one
segment of twelve, beat the best constant write at $\alpha\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.15,0.25\}$ and
lost to it at $\alpha\in\{0.5,1.0\}$, a clean saturation crossover. See
\texttt{results/txc\_wins/geometrystruqposx\_lowdose.json}.} A single scalar dose is therefore
not comparable across procedures, and dose--response curves should be reported instead.

\textbf{The three-line version.} V7 and \texttt{txc\_flat} are the same intervention, one
published as the method and one built as the null. PP is a convolution of the slab with the
latent's activation profile, and reduces to the same constant write wherever that activation is
flat. \texttt{txc\_slab} is the only one of the four that delivers $\ac$ to the residual stream.

\section{The AC part is not a technicality: a measured case}
\label{sec:measured}

The decomposition above is algebra; whether $\ac$ carries any usable effect is empirical. On the
one task in this sprint where a crosscoder cleanly beats every learned per-token arm --- the
Lost-in-the-Middle retrieved-document-position cell, gold at $0$ versus gold at $4$ --- it carries
essentially \emph{all} of it. Median over three dictionary initialisations, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
layer 14, $T=11$, held-out content, every write at identical injected Frobenius norm
(\texttt{results/txc\_wins/litm\_0v4\_tr\_tsaep\_ds\{0,1,2\}.json}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
arm & matched $|\alpha|=0.5$ & peak over grid\\
\midrule
\texttt{txc\_slab} \quad(writes DC $+$ AC)          & $\mathbf{0.843}$ & $\mathbf{2.493}$\\
\texttt{tsaep\_broadcast} (published T-SAE)          & $0.238$ & $0.257$\\
\texttt{sae\_broadcast} (TopK SAE)                   & $0.124$ & $1.474$\\
\texttt{tsae\_broadcast} (attention tSAE)            & $0.100$ & $0.118$\\
\texttt{txc\_flat} \quad(DC only $\;\equiv\;$ V7)    & $0.044$ & $0.180$\\
\texttt{random\_slab} \quad(null)                    & $0.048$ & $0.182$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\texttt{txc\_flat} is the V7 write up to a scalar (Proposition~\ref{prop:v7-flat}), and it measures
$0.044$ against a \emph{random} slab's $0.048$ --- indistinguishable, at matched dose and again at
peak ($0.180$ versus $0.182$). The same latent, writing its full slab, measures $0.843$: a factor
of roughly $19$.

\begin{center}
\emph{On this cell the paper's steering protocol would have scored the crosscoder at the
random-write level. The DC component of the winning latent is worth nothing; the entire effect
lives in $\ac$, which V7 averages away.}
\end{center}

This is one cell, one model and one metric, and it does not establish that the same holds on the
paper's tasks. What it does establish is that the gap between the protocols is not a small
correction: a latent can be genuinely useful and completely invisible to a window-averaging
intervention. Any negative steering result obtained under V7 is therefore uninformative about
whether the latent could have steered --- which is a different and much weaker statement than
those results are usually read as making.

\section*{Provenance and what is not established here}

The V7 and PP descriptions are read from
\verb|origin/temp-bench-anon:src/temp_bench/case_studies/steering.py| and the protocol default
from \verb|SteeringConfig| (\verb|protocol: str = "v7"|) and
\verb|experiments/c5_steering/run.py| (\verb|--protocol|, \verb|default="v7"|), corroborated by
\verb|experiments/c5_steering/README.md| (``V7-tile-broadcast'' pipeline). \emph{The manuscript
itself has not been checked.} If a published figure was produced with \verb|--protocol pp|, that
figure belongs in the second column rather than the first.

Propositions \ref{prop:v7-flat} and \ref{prop:pp} are algebraic consequences of decoder linearity
and hold exactly. What is \emph{not} established here is empirical: how large
$\mathrm{var}(c_w)$ is in practice, and therefore how far PP sits from flat on real prompts;
and whether the $\ac$ component carries useful effect on the paper's tasks, which requires
running \texttt{txc\_slab}-style writes on them rather than reasoning about the protocol.
