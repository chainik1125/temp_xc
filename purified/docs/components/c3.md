## Hypothesis

On standard SAEBench-style sparse-probing tasks, the temporal architectures
(TXC-base, TXC-pro, T-SAE, MLC) match or beat the per-token TopK-SAE
baseline at every probe sparsity, with the gap widening as more features
are admitted. TXC-pro's matryoshka + contrastive loss should give it a
small lead at the most-sparse regime (k=5) where feature quality matters
more than feature count.

**Result** (SAEBench-36): confirmed at the family level. At peak
($k_{\mathrm{feat}}\!=\!160$) MLC leads at 0.959, then a tight cluster
of TXC-base $T\!\in\!\{20, 10, 5\}$ (0.955-0.956) and T-SAE (0.952) /
TXC-pro (0.952), with TopK-SAE behind at 0.941 — a **+0.014-0.018 AUC
gap from the temporal family to the per-token TopK baseline**, well
outside σ_seeds (≤0.001 at $k\!\geq\!80$). At $k_{\mathrm{feat}}\!=\!5$
TXC-pro's matryoshka helps (0.869 vs TXC-base $T\!=\!5$ at 0.859 and
TopK-SAE at 0.851), but MLC is ahead at 0.874; TXC-pro vs MLC overlaps
in seed-σ (±0.015). T-modulation on TXC-base shows a small monotonic
trend at $k\!\leq\!40$ (T=5 < T=10 < T=20 by ~0.002-0.004 per doubling)
that **flattens at $k\!\geq\!80$** where all three T variants converge
to within 0.001 AUC of each other. TFA underperforms by 0.07-0.18 AUC
across the sweep, well outside seed-σ.

## Setup

- **Subject model**: `google/gemma-2-2b-it` layer 13 residual stream.
  Same activation cache as C4, C5 (single source of truth keyed by
  `act_cache_key`).
- **Activation cache**: 24K FineWeb sequences × 128 tokens, fp16
  per-layer. The cache is keyed by `act_cache_key` and pushed to
  `han1823123123/temp-bench-data` so the framework's
  `scripts/sync_from_hf.sh` can warm-start fresh ephemeral pods
  without rebuilding. MLC uses a separate 5-layer cache
  (`gemma_2_2b_it_l11to15_fineweb_24k128`) covering layers L11-L15.
- **Architectures (locked, no hill-climbing)** — six baseline families
  trained at literature-faithful per-arch TrainingConfig (decisions § 15
  + § 16):
  - `topk_sae` (vanilla TopK, no temporal): B=1024, `train_window_size=1`
    → 1024 tok/step (SAEBench App. B canonical).
  - `tsae_paper` (T-SAE, paper-faithful BatchTopK + temporal contrastive):
    B=1024, `train_window_size=2` → 2048 tok/step (Bhalla/Ye §3.1
    adjacent pairs).
  - `tfa` (Transformer Feature Assignment, attention over the full
    sequence): B=32, `train_window_size=None` → 4096 tok/step (Phase 7
    `train_phase7.py:312-353` reference; B=32 keeps the attention tensor
    tractable at d_sae=18432).
  - `mlc` (multi-layer crosscoder, paper-faithful 5-layer L=11–15):
    B=1024, multi-layer cache → 5120 tok/step. agent_em_100k owns the
    multi-layer cache build + the C3 MLC sweep.
  - `txc_base` (TXC-base, vanilla TopK temporal crosscoder T=5): B=1024,
    samples 1 random T=5 window per row internally → 5120 tok/step.
  - `txc_pro` (TXC-pro, subseq T_max=10 + matryoshka + multi-distance
    contrastive): B=1024, samples internally → 5120 tok/step.
  All archs train for the same n_steps=20_000 (decisions § 12 — Han
  deadline override 2026-05-04 PM); within-component fairness invariant
  is "every arch trains for 20K steps at its source paper's intended
  setup." All archs at training $k_{\mathrm{pos}}\!=\!20$ (matched to
  T-SAE paper).
- **TXC-base T-sweep** (decisions § 17, Han 2026-05-05 PM): TXC-base
  trains 3× — at $T \in \{5, 10, 20\}$ — to test whether longer
  temporal context monotonically improves probing. Other archs use
  their canonical T (above) and are not swept.
- **Probing protocol** — Phase 7 S-tail + mean-pool, fixed across
  all archs (`temp_bench.eval.probing.s_tail_probe`):
  1. **Activation cache** ($S\!=\!32$ tail, left-aligned). For each task
     prompt $i$ we cache the last $S\!=\!32$ token activations of the
     subject model at the C3 layer, shape $(N, S, d_{\mathrm{in}})$.
     Tokenization is **right-padded** at build time, then per example
     re-sliced into a 32-frame so the *real* tokens occupy positions
     $[\text{first\_real}_i,\ S\!-\!1]$ and zero-padding sits at the
     start. The per-example `first_real[i]` index is stored alongside
     the cache so downstream pooling can mask the padding region.
     (See `temp_bench.data.nlp.probe_cache::_encode_texts` for the
     right-pad-then-reslice fix that landed in 2026-05-04 — older
     v1.0.0 caches with naive right-padding are kept for backward-compat
     diff but excluded from the headline.)
  2. **SAE forward**: each arch encodes the cached $(N, S, d_{\mathrm{in}})$
     tensor → SAE latents $(N, S, d_{\mathrm{sae}})$. For window archs
     ($T\!>\!1$: TXC-base, TXC-pro, T-SAE) the window starts at
     $\text{first\_real}_i$ and slides forward by 1; the validity rule
     $S \!\geq\! T$ guarantees ≥1 valid window per row. Edge-case rows
     where $n_{\mathrm{real}}\!<\!T$ fall back to the all-windows mean
     (small fraction of winogrande/wsc; per the 36-task headline these
     are excluded from the final score anyway).
  3. **Mean-pool over the token axis**, masking positions before
     `first_real[i]` so padding contributes zero. Yields a single
     $(d_{\mathrm{sae}},)$ feature vector per prompt — same dimension
     for every arch (matched-sparsity comparison, no implicit
     re-architecting at probe time).
  4. **Top-k feature selection** on the train split only: compute
     $|\mu_{y=1} - \mu_{y=0}|$ across the $d_{\mathrm{sae}}$ axis and
     keep the top $k_{\mathrm{feat}}$ indices. **No CV / no peeking at
     test set during selection.**
  5. **Probe**: scikit-learn `LogisticRegression(penalty='l1',
     solver='liblinear', C=1.0, max_iter=1000, random_state=0)` fitted
     on those $k_{\mathrm{feat}}$ features. L1 + liblinear matches the
     Kantamneni / SAEBench convention.
  6. **Metric**: per-task ROC-AUC on the held-out test split
     (`sklearn.metrics.roc_auc_score`). Headline is the unweighted
     mean across the 36 SAEBench tasks.
  - **Sample sizes** (built once per IT-L13 cache, deterministic):
    $n_{\mathrm{train}} \in [443,\ 4000]$ and $n_{\mathrm{test}} \in
    [111,\ 1000]$ per task, depending on dataset size. The per-task
    train/test split is the upstream SAEBench-defined one (no
    re-shuffling per seed) — seed variance enters via the SAE training
    seed, not the probe split.
  - **Sweep axis**: $k_{\mathrm{feat}} \in \{5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160,
    320, 640\}$ (8-point sweep, Han 2026-05-06). Each arch × seed
    cell encodes once and probes 8×.
- **Task suite — `SAEBench` (n=36)**. The canonical upstream SAEBench
  sparse-probing suite (Karvonen et al.; 36 binary one-vs-rest tasks
  across 8 datasets), composition fixed by `chosen_classes_per_dataset`
  in upstream `sae_bench/sae_bench_utils/dataset_info.py`:

    | dataset | classes | n |
    |---|---|---|
    | `LabHC/bias_in_bios_class_set1` | `0,1,2,6,9` | 5 |
    | `LabHC/bias_in_bios_class_set2` | `11,13,14,18,19` | 5 |
    | `LabHC/bias_in_bios_class_set3` | `20,21,22,25,26` | 5 |
    | `canrager/amazon_reviews_mcauley_1and5` | `1,2,3,5,6` | 5 |
    | `canrager/amazon_reviews_mcauley_1and5_sentiment` | `1.0,5.0` | 2 |
    | `codeparrot/github-code` | `C,Python,HTML,Java,PHP` | 5 |
    | `fancyzhx/ag_news` | `0,1,2,3` | 4 |
    | `Helsinki-NLP/europarl` | `en,fr,de,es,nl` | 5 |
    | **SAEBench total** | | **36** |

  Two cross-token tasks (`winogrande_correct_completion`,
  `wsc_coreference`) were eval'd alongside SAEBench during the sweep
  and are stored in the leaderboard as `auc__winogrande_*` /
  `auc__wsc_*` for completeness, **but are dropped from the headline
  metric** (per-token mean-pool aggregation can't score them well —
  they require explicit cross-token reasoning, an architectural axis
  separate from the SAE feature quality this component measures). See
  Caveats. Re-introducing them is a one-line `analysis.py` change if
  needed.

  Three implementation deltas vs the wasteland 36-task loader were
  landed during framework setup (now stable in `temp_bench.eval.probing`):
  `codeparrot/github-code` replaces `code_search_net` and pulls C +
  Python + HTML + Java + PHP via the canonical SAEBench loader
  (requires `trust_remote_code=True` and the `datasets<4` pin in
  `pyproject.toml`); the amazon sentiment task adds the 1.0-vs-rest
  binary; the amazon categories task switched from streaming top-5
  to a deterministic non-streaming pull that guarantees the
  `["1","2","3","5","6"]` classes are populated.
- **Seeds**: {1, 2, 42}.
- **Hardware**: 1× H100 for cache + training (one fresh act_cache build
  ~3 H100-hr; per-arch training ~25-90 min depending on T; probing eval
  cache-hits on training and runs ~5-30 min per cell).

## Caveats

- **Per-arch TrainingConfig**: every arch trains at its source paper's
  intended setup (see Setup; per-step token throughput varies). The
  alternative — uniform per-step tokens across archs — would either
  starve TFA's attention of context or over-batch the per-token
  baselines (decisions § 15). Within-component fairness invariant:
  same $n_{\mathrm{steps}}\!=\!20{,}000$ × same activation cache.
- **σ_seeds dominates the gap at k=5**; cross-arch claims at k=5 must
  be "competitive within seed-σ", not "beats X". The interesting
  comparisons live at $k_{\mathrm{feat}}\!\geq\!80$ where σ_seeds drops
  below 0.002.
- **Headline excludes cross-token tasks** (`winogrande_correct_completion`,
  `wsc_coreference`). Those two tasks require cross-token reasoning
  (resolving a referent that lives many tokens away from the variant
  word); a per-token mean-pool encoder cannot represent the relevant
  contrast and every arch in the sweep scored 0.40-0.50 on them
  regardless of architecture. Including them in the mean dragged
  every arch down by roughly the same ~0.027 AUC at $k_{\mathrm{feat}}\!=\!20$
  (uniform shift, no rank flips, except TFA which was already low).
  Their contribution is therefore **architecture-orthogonal noise on
  this benchmark**, not signal we want in a feature-quality headline.
  Per-task AUCs for both remain in `results/leaderboard.jsonl` under
  `auc__winogrande_correct_completion` / `auc__wsc_coreference`;
  flipping the two CT tasks back into the headline is a one-line
  `analysis.py` change (`CT_TASKS = ()`).
- **TFA underperforms at every k_feat** by 0.07-0.18 AUC. We attribute
  this to the small B=32 batch (paper-faithful per Phase 7
  `train_phase7.py:312-353`); a fairer comparison at B=1024 would need
  a re-architected attention head (the (B, T, d_in)·(B, T, d_sae)
  attention tensor goes O(B²) at d_sae=18432). Marked as a known
  limitation; not a TFA-vs-TXC headline.
- **Pre-canonical cells stay on disk for diff** (older batch=256 / no
  T-sweep cells with stale `train_key` values). The aggregator filters
  via `canonical_train_keys()` so they don't pollute the headline; they
  remain in `results/leaderboard.jsonl` for backward-compat queries.

### Switching subject model or layer

The framework caches at three levels:
1. activation cache keyed by `(subject_model, layer, dataset, n_seqs, seq_len)`
2. trained-model cache keyed by `(arch, arch_version, hparams, seed, train_data_key)`
3. eval-result cache keyed by `(train_key, eval_protocol_version, eval_cfg)`

Switching `subject_model: gemma-2-2b-it → gemma-2-2b` invalidates only
(1) and the downstream re-evaluations; arch implementations and code
versions are untouched. This is by design so we can run a "BASE-side
extension" experiment after the IT-side is locked, without touching the
core paper claims.

## Reproduction

```bash
cd $(git rev-parse --show-toplevel)/purified
source scripts/set_agent_env.sh agent_nlp
bash scripts/sync_from_hf.sh    # warm-start cache + checkpoints

# Six-arch canonical sweep + TXC-base T-sweep
TQDM_DISABLE=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.c3_probing.run \
    --seeds 1 2 42 \
    --k-feats 5 10 20 40 80 160 320 640
TQDM_DISABLE=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.c3_probing_txc_T_sweep.run \
    --T-values 10 20 --seeds 1 2 42 \
    --k-feats 5 10 20 40 80 160 320 640

# Re-render this file's AUTO-RESULTS block + plot:
TQDM_DISABLE=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.c3_probing.analysis
```

The runner is idempotent: every cell is keyed by `(train_key, eval_key)`
and skipped if already in the leaderboard. A fresh run on cached caches
+ HF-resident checkpoints completes in ~3 hr (eval-only).
