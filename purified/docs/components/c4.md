## Hypothesis

On the qualitative / passage-discrimination axis, TXC-pro Pareto-dominates
T-SAE: it achieves comparable or higher SEMANTIC labels in the top-k
features ranked by per-token variance (the original T-SAE metric) while
also winning on probing AUC (C3).

## Setup

- **Subject model**: `google/gemma-2-2b-it` layer 13 residual stream.
  (Note: IT, not BASE — matches T-SAE paper § 4.2.)
- **Architectures**: T-SAE (paper config), TXC-base (T=5), TXC-pro
  (T_max=10). + one Stacked-SAE control.
- **Concat datasets**: `concat_A`, `concat_B`, `concat_random` —
  N=3–7 contiguous passages on different topics, packed without padding.
- **Three qualitative metrics** (kept for ablation honesty):
  1. `semantic_count` — top-32 by per-token variance, Haiku-judge SEMANTIC vs SYNTACTIC
  2. `semantic_count_pdvar` — top-32 by Var(per-passage mean activation)
  3. paper-style passage-ID probe — k-sparse multinomial logreg, k=5, 5-fold CV
- **Cache reuse**: shares the C3 cache when feasible. C3 uses BASE; C4 uses IT.
  *That's two caches.* If we drop one, prefer to keep IT for both — T-SAE
  paper used IT, so reproducing their numbers is cleaner.
- **Seeds**: {1, 2, 42}.

## Caveats

- Phase 6 had three different qualitative metrics that ranked archs
  differently. The paper must report all three, and lead with the metric
  most directly comparable to T-SAE's published claim (var-ranked).
- `pdvar` re-ranks Cycle-F-style BatchTopK archs upward — this is real
  signal, not metric-shopping, but discuss carefully.
- Haiku judge is a black box; document the prompt + temperature + judge agreement
  CSV (see C7 for blind-judge CSV pattern).
