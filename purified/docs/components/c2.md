## Hypothesis

Per-token SAEs recover the features they *see* on each token. When
the data-generating process has hidden structure beyond what a single
token reveals, SAEs miss it. Temporal architectures that aggregate
across positions can recover that hidden structure.

C2 tests this in five synthetic regimes:

- **Setup A — coupled features**: emissions are deterministic OR-gates
  of K hidden Markov chains. Hidden = the K hidden chain directions;
  per-token SAEs recover the M emission directions and miss the
  hidden K.
- **Setup B — noisy emissions**: emissions are stochastic Bernoulli
  observations of independent Markov chains. Hidden = the noise-free
  underlying state; per-token SAEs track the noisy observation, while
  temporal models can *denoise* by aggregating multiple observations
  of the same hidden state.
- **Setup C — ρ-sweep on Setup A**: vary the temporal autocorrelation
  ρ to disambiguate Effect 1 (sample aggregation, ρ-independent) from
  Effect 2 (temporal pattern detection, ρ-dependent) per Dmitry's
  framing.
- **Setup D — Noisy + overlap**: Dmitry-style coupled-noisy generator
  combines OR-gate coupling (Setup A) with per-token Bernoulli
  emission noise (Setup B) AND dense parent overlap (n_parents up to
  10). The noisy + overlap combination produces the regime where
  TXC's gAUC win is largest and most robust across k_pos.
- **Setup E — Hierarchical bench (engineered)**: K_g slow globals
  modulate K_l fast locals through binary parent assignments. Globals
  contribute orthogonal directions in observation space. Engineered
  test of whether TXC selectively recovers global directions while
  per-token SAEs prefer the abundant local directions.
- **Setup F — Coupled + observation noise**: Setup A's deterministic
  OR-gate coupling with additive Gaussian noise σ on the observation.
  Isolates the denoising axis (no Bernoulli emission noise, no dense
  overlap). Tests Setup-B-style denoising on COUPLED features.
- **Setup G — Hierarchical + observation noise**: Setup E (slow
  globals × fast modulated locals) with additive Gaussian noise σ.
  Tests whether the global-vs-local divide AND the denoising story
  interact constructively in one setup.

Both setups share the metric framework (decoder AUC, single-latent
correlation, linear probe R²) but apply it to different ground-truth
targets.

## Setup B — Noisy independent emissions

20 independent Markov chains $h_i(t)$ with stochastic Bernoulli
emissions: when $h_i = 1$ the observation $s_i$ fires with
probability $p_B = 0.625$ ($\gamma = 0.25$), and never fires when
$h_i = 0$. **No coupling** — each feature has its own chain. Tests
whether temporal models recover the underlying hidden state $h_i$
from the noisy observation $s_i$.

| Knob | Value |
|---|---|
| n_features                | 20 |
| ρ, π                      | 0.7, 0.5 |
| p_A, p_B (γ)              | 0, 0.625 (0.25) |
| d (residual), d_sae       | 40, 40 |
| seq_len T                 | 64 |
| Models                    | TFA-pos, Stacked T={2,5}, TXC-base T={2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12}, TXC-pro T_max=10 |
| k sweep                   | {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20} (auto-skipped where $k_{\rm pos} \cdot T > d_{\rm sae}$) |
| Seeds                     | {1, 2, 42} |

**Datasource**: `toy_markov_n20_d40_noisy`.
**Internal component name**: `c1_noisy` (preserved for leaderboard
provenance; logically part of C2).
**Single ground truth**: 20 feature directions in $\mathbb{R}^{40}$.
**Headline metrics**: AUC (decoder alignment), single-latent
correlation ratio $\bar{r}_{\rm global}/\bar{r}_{\rm local}$ and
linear probe ratio $R^2_{\rm global}/R^2_{\rm local}$ (denoising).

> **Why no eAUC / gAUC for Setup B.** Unlike Setups A / D / E, this
> generator has only **one** feature-direction set $\{f_i\}_{i=1}^{20}$:
> activations are built as $x = \sum_i s_i \cdot |m_i| \cdot f_i$ where
> the noisy emission $s_i \in \{0, 1\}$ and the clean hidden state
> $h_i \in \{0, 1\}$ share the **same** direction $f_i$ (see
> `src/temp_bench/data/toy/markov.py:91-123`). A geometric
> direction-recovery AUC against "emission features" and against
> "hidden features" is therefore the **same number** by construction
> — there is no separate hidden basis to align with. The local-vs-global
> distinction in Setup B lives at the **latent-code level** (does $z$
> fire on $s_i$ or on $h_i$?), captured by the single-latent correlation
> $\bar{r}_{\rm local}$ vs $\bar{r}_{\rm global}$ and the linear-probe
> $R^2_{\rm local}$ vs $R^2_{\rm global}$ shown in the denoising scatters
> below — those play the role that eAUC vs gAUC plays in Setups A / D / E.

## Setup C — ρ-sweep (Effect 1 vs Effect 2)

In response to a question raised by Dmitry: does TXC's gAUC win on
Setup A reflect *temporal pattern detection* (Effect 2 — requires
ρ > 0) or merely *sample aggregation* (Effect 1 — pooling T tokens
reduces TopK selection variance, works at any ρ including 0)? We
sweep ρ ∈ {0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.7, 0.9} on the headline trio, holding
all other knobs fixed at Setup A values.

| Knob | Value |
|---|---|
| Datasources | `toy_coupled_K10_M20_d256_rho{00,03,06,09}` (+ legacy ρ=0.7) |
| Archs       | TopK-SAE, TXC-base T=5, TXC-pro T=2 (headline trio) |
| k_pos       | {1, 5} |
| Seeds       | {1, 2, 42} |

**Decision rule**:
- gAUC roughly **flat** across ρ → Effect 1 (sample aggregation;
  weak temporal claim — paper must reframe).
- gAUC **grows** with ρ → Effect 2 (temporal pattern detection;
  strong claim defensible).

construction:

- K_g = 10 **slow** global Markov chains (ρ_g = 0.95, π_g = 0.05).

## Setup L — Magnitude-modulated locals (no globals in obs space)

agent_pro mission 2026-05-07. The **purest** Effect-2 test we have:
globals are NOT in the observation space at all.

- K_g = 10 slow Markov globals (ρ_g = 0.95, π_g = 0.05).
- K_l = 30 locals fire i.i.d. with constant probability p_l = 0.5
  (firing INDEPENDENT of global state — verified by smoke test:
  cov(local_fire, parent_on) = 0).
- Local MAGNITUDE is modulated by the parent global:
  ``mag(j, t) = (1 + α · 1[parent of j is on]) ·
  folded_normal(μ=1, σ=0.15)`` with α = 1.
- Observation: ``x(t) = Σ_j s_l[j,t] · mag(j,t) · f_l[j]``.
  No global-direction term: ``<x, f_g> = 0`` exactly (verified by
  smoke test).

What this means: per-token observation is a sum of magnitude-jittered
local firings. Linear projection onto f_g is identically zero. The
ONLY way to recover globals is to detect the slow temporal
modulation of local-firing magnitudes — which requires aggregating
across multiple tokens. TXC's window pool sees the slow trend;
per-token SAE sees only individual magnitudes (looks like high-
variance noise).

| TXC-base | T=5 | 0.046 | 0.772 |
| TXC-base | T=12 | 0.080 | 0.622 |

ALL architectures fail gAUC — none recover the f_g directions. The

2× when parent on) is too subtle relative to the iid magnitude

L (α=1) is **dropped** as a TXC-favorable gAUC regime. Follow-up:
**OBELISK** retests the same mechanism with α=5 and p_l=0.05
(rare, dramatic amplitude bursts) — that is the canonical
high-SNR magnitude-modulation regime; at α=5 the signal-to-noise
ratio is much more favourable for window-pool detection.

## Setup PHALANX — Period-locked global pulses (deterministic phase)

agent_pro follow-up to Setup K + L (mission 2026-05-07). The most
literal phase-detection test we can construct: globals fire on a
strict deterministic period. No Markov stochastics, no probabilistic
modulation — just a pure phase signal embedded in the magnitude
distribution of locals.

- K_g = 10 globals; global k is on at token t iff
  ``(t + φ_k) mod τ < δ`` where τ is the period (default 8) and δ is
  the duty cycle (default 1 → exactly 1/τ tokens on per global).
  φ_k is a per-global random phase offset in {0, ..., τ-1}, fixed
  across all sequences.
- K_l = 30 locals fire iid p_l = 0.5 (independent of globals).
- Local magnitude: ``mag = (1 + α · 1[parent_on]) · folded_normal``.
- Observation: ``x(t) = Σ_j s_l[j,t] · mag(j,t) · f_l[j]``. NO global
  direction in observation.

**Why this is interesting**: a per-token SAE sees a marginal
distribution that is identical at every token (parent_on is uniform
in expectation). It cannot distinguish phase. A window of size T
matters mathematically: only T ≥ τ can in principle resolve the
period. Predicts a SHARP threshold in TXC gAUC at T = τ, in
contrast to Setup L's smooth monotone rise.

| Knob | Value |
|---|---|
| Generator | ``temp_bench.data.toy.phalanx:phalanx_features`` |
| K_g, K_l | 10, 30 |
| n_global_parents | 1 |
| period τ | 8 (one of the swept T values) |
| duty_cycle | 1 (each global on for 1 of every 8 tokens) |
| p_l (firing prob) | 0.5 (constant; independent of globals) |
| α (magnitude boost) | 1.0 |
| d, d_sae | 256, 40 |
| Models | TopK, Stacked T={2, 5}, T-SAE, TFA-pos, TXC-base T ∈ {2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12} |
| k_pos sweep | {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8} (capped by k×T ≤ d_sae=40) |
| Seeds | {1, 2, 42} |
| n_steps | 8 000 |

**Datasource**: ``toy_phalanx_Kg10_Kl30_d256_period8``.

## Setup OBELISK — Rare + amplified magnitude-mod

agent_pro follow-up to Setup K + L (mission 2026-05-07). A Setup-L
variant that concentrates the signal in rare, dramatic events.

- K_g = 10 slow Markov globals (ρ_g = 0.95, π_g = 0.05; same as L).
- K_l = 30 locals fire iid p_l = 0.05 (rare; ~3 firings per
  seq_len=64 token sequence per local).
- Local magnitude: ``mag = (1 + α · 1[parent_on]) · folded_normal``
  with α = 5 (so a firing with parent_on has 6× the magnitude of one
  with parent_off — a dramatic amplitude burst).
- Observation: ``x(t) = Σ_j s_l[j,t] · mag(j,t) · f_l[j]``. NO global
  direction in observation.

**Why this matters**: Setup L (p_l = 0.5, α = 1) has dense, gentle
magnitude modulation — every token has ~15 active locals, modulated
by ~50%. The signal is detectable but spread thin. OBELISK
concentrates the signal: most tokens are pure noise; a few scattered
tokens have huge bursts that encode global state. Tests whether TXC
can correctly attribute amplitude bursts to a slow-changing global
rather than treating them as outlier locals. Predicts: TXC gAUC
depends on whether T contains enough firings to detect amplitude
trend; per-token SAE gAUC ≈ chance.

|---|---|
| Generator | ``temp_bench.data.toy.obelisk:obelisk_features`` |

| n_global_parents | 1 |
| ρ_g, π_g | 0.95, 0.05 (slow chains) |
| p_l (firing prob) | **0.05** (rare; vs 0.5 in Setup L) |
| α (magnitude boost) | **5.0** (vs 1.0 in Setup L — 6× amplitude on parent_on) |
| d, d_sae | 256, 40 |
| Models | TopK, Stacked T={2, 5}, T-SAE, TFA-pos, TXC-base T ∈ {2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12} |
| k_pos sweep | {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8} (capped by k×T ≤ d_sae=40) |

## Caveats

- **Setup A**: gAUC measures dictionary direction alignment. The
  linear probe $R^2$ ratio is ≈ 1 for almost all archs — global
  *information* is present in any sufficient dictionary. The paper
  claim is specifically about dictionary alignment, not information
  sufficiency.
- **Setup B**: a parameter mismatch with the wasteland source shifts
  the per-token "no denoising" floor up; absolute denoising ratios
  are shifted but the qualitative tier separation (TXC > floor,
  others = floor) reproduces. Detailed parameter accounting and
  cross-checks against wasteland numbers in `c2_wasteland_audit.md`.
- **Setup C**: agent_paper's prior T-modulation analysis already
  hints at Effect 1 (gAUC at k=5 drops as T grows: T=2→0.904,
  T=5→0.684, T=12→0.678 — temporal context HURTS, opposite of
  Effect-2 prediction). The ρ-sweep is confirmation evidence on the
  orthogonal axis.
- **Setup D**: at our d_sae = 40 cap, T=5 archs cannot run beyond
  k_pos = 8 (k_win = k_pos × T must ≤ d_sae or `topk` fails). HUNT
  k_pos values 10, 15, 20 crash the txc_base subprocess by design;
  ZOOM caps at k_pos = 8. n_steps = 8000 is sufficient for
  convergence at d=256, d_sae=40 — initial 30k attempt was abandoned
  because contention (50+ concurrent processes) made each cell take
  ~10 min wall. The mid-flight cutover means leaderboard cells
  before `2026-05-06T22:54:30Z` use n_steps=30k and are filtered
  out of the Setup D plots; only post-cutover cells (n_steps=8k)
  are shown. The change shifts SAE's k_pos=1 gAUC slightly relative
  to the HUNT-phase-table numbers above (HUNT was at n_steps=20k);
  the qualitative TXC > SAE pattern is unaffected.
- **Setup E**: at d_sae = 40 = K_g + K_l, SAE's dictionary capacity
  exactly matches the feature count. At high k_pos, SAE can recover
  all features; the global-vs-local divide is most visible at low
  k_pos (k = 1-4). A future pass with d_sae = 80 (touches
  `configs/locked_archs.yaml` — agent_paper's territory) would
  extend the divide further along the k-axis.
