\section{Backtracking Case Study: Full Details}
\label{app:c7}

This appendix supplies the methodological details that underlie \cref{sec:backtracking}.

\subsection{Architectures and training configuration}
\label{app:c7-archs}

We compare six architectures, all trained on the same Llama-3.1-8B residual-stream activation cache at layer $10$ with $d_{\text{SAE}} = 32{,}768$ for $n_{\text{steps}} = 300{,}000$ at batch size $1024$: a per-token TopK SAE \cite{gao2024scaling}, the temporal SAE of \citet{bhalla2025tsae} in its paper-faithful configuration, the temporal feature analyzer (TFA) of \citet{lubana2025priors}, a multi-layer crosscoder (MLC) \cite{lindsey2024sparsecrosscoders}, our TXC-base ($T = 5$, $k_{\text{pos}} = 20$), and our TXC-pro ($T_{\max} = 10$, $t_{\text{sample}} = 5$, $k_{\text{pos}} = 20$, Matryoshka with eight nested groups, multi-distance contrastive at shifts $\Delta \in \{1, 2\}$). The two TXC architectures are additionally trained at batch size $256$ to disambiguate batch-size effects from architecture effects; the additional batch-size cells are reported in \cref{app:c7-bs-256}.

\subsection{Stage A reproduction}
\label{app:c7-stage-a}

``Stage~A'' here refers to the data preparation phase that produces the labeled-sentence dataset and the difference-of-means steering direction. (The terms ``Stage~A'' and ``Stage~B'' are our internal labels for the two phases of the pipeline; they do not appear in the cited prior work.) The dataset construction recipe follows \citet{venhoff2025steering}, as replicated in \citet[Appendix C]{ward2025reasoning}: (1) generate $300$ math-style prompts in ten problem-domain categories using Claude Sonnet~$3.7$, (2) generate reasoning traces with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, (3) classify each sentence in each trace by an LLM judge. The ten problem-domain categories are \texttt{basic\_logic}, \texttt{geometry}, \texttt{probability}, \texttt{arithmetic}, \texttt{counting}, \texttt{number\_theory}, \texttt{set\_theory}, \texttt{sequences}, \texttt{inequalities}, \texttt{algebra\_word\_problems} ($30$ prompts each); $280$ of the $300$ form the \texttt{dom} split used to build the steering direction and $20$ are held out as the \texttt{eval} split. The sentence-classification step in \cite{venhoff2025steering, ward2025reasoning} uses GPT-4o; we substitute Claude Sonnet~$4.6$ for consistency with the inducement-judge in \cref{app:c7-judge}. The labels in our pipeline are binary (\texttt{is\_backtracking}); the segmentation grammar and the labeling prompt are reproduced in \cref{app:c7-judge}.

The steering vector is the difference-of-means direction-of-meaning vector for the \texttt{is\_backtracking} label, computed at residual stream layer~$10$ from a window of token positions offset $-13$ through $-8$ relative to the start of each labeled backtracking sentence. Both the layer-$10$ choice and the negative-offset window are from \citet[Sec.\ 3.1, Appendix B.1]{ward2025reasoning}: a sweep across all $32$ layers identifies layer~$10$ as the most effective hookpoint, and an offset sweep identifies $\sim$$-13$ to $-8$ as the offset range that maximizes induced backtracking. \citet{ward2025reasoning} also derive the steering vector from \emph{base-model} activations rather than reasoning-model activations, which is the choice we adopt as well.

\subsection{Cohort construction}
\label{app:c7-cohort}

The cohort comprises $61$ MATH-500 questions \cite{hendrycks2021math} partitioned into two strata. The truly-wrong stratum ($n = 31$) consists of questions on which the unsteered reasoning model produces a parsable boxed answer that does not match the ground truth under LaTeX-aware comparison. The originally-correct stratum ($n = 30$) consists of questions on which the unsteered model produces a parsable answer matching the ground truth. Questions on which the model exceeds the token budget without producing a boxed answer are dropped, since both inducement and detection metrics require a parsable continuation. Within strata, questions are drawn deterministically from a \texttt{flip\_matrix.parquet} reference produced once on the locked datasource and shared across all architectures, ensuring that every architecture is evaluated on the same $61$ questions in the same order. Question identifiers follow the MATH-500 \texttt{unique\_id} schema (e.g.\ \texttt{test/algebra/1184.json}).

\subsection{Magnitude grid}
\label{app:c7-grid}

We use a non-uniform grid of $25$ magnitudes,
\[
    \mathcal{M} = \{0, \pm0.5, \pm1, \pm2, \pm3, \pm4, \pm5, \pm6, \pm7, \pm8, \pm10, \pm12, \pm16\},
\]
densified between $\pm 0.5$ and $\pm 8$ where the inducement curves transition (and where the gradient of $\Delta gc$ with respect to $m$ is largest), and sparser at the tails where the curves saturate. The zero magnitude corresponds to no intervention and serves as the per-question baseline in \cref{eq:delta-gc}.

\subsection{cut25 protocol details}
\label{app:c7-cut25}

For each cohort question $q$ we obtain the unsteered Stage~A continuation tokens $t_q^{\text{full}}$ and define the prefix as the first $\lfloor 0.25 \cdot |t_q^{\text{full}}| \rfloor$ tokens. Conditioned on this prefix, the reasoning model resamples a continuation at temperature $1.0$ with a token budget of $1024$ while the steering hook adds $m \cdot v_{\text{feat}}$ to the layer-$10$ residual stream at every position, where $v_{\text{feat}}$ is the architecture-specific mined direction (rescaled to match the $\ell_2$ norm of the dom-base-union vector to ensure cross-architecture magnitude comparability) and $m \in \mathcal{M}$ is the panel-level magnitude. The resampled tokens form the continuation that the Sonnet judge scores.

We adopt cut25 over alternatives (full-trace continuation, LLM-judged-cut at the first sentence boundary preceding the original backtracking event) on the basis of pilot experiments showing that a fixed proportional cut yields the most consistent measurement of the inducement metric across questions of varying length. Full-trace continuation under-discriminates between architectures because the first part of the trace is typically dominated by problem restatement; LLM-judged-cut introduces an additional source of judge noise that we wished to control for separately.

\subsection{Sonnet judge prompt and reliability}
\label{app:c7-judge}

The inducement metric $\Delta gc$ (\cref{eq:delta-gc}) requires, for each $(\text{architecture}, \text{question}, \text{magnitude})$ panel, a count $gc(a, q, m)$ of genuine backtracking events in the resampled continuation. The judge rubric distinguishes genuine backtracking (catching an arithmetic error and recomputing, noticing a missing problem constraint, rejecting the current approach in favor of a different method, re-evaluating a wrong assumption) from filler and pseudo-backtracking (conversational ``wait,'' restating the same conclusion, looped emissions). The prompt presented to Sonnet~$4.6$ (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, max output $200$ tokens, default sampling temperature) is reproduced verbatim:

\begin{quote}\small
You are evaluating a math problem-solving generation for genuine backtracking behavior.

Background: backtracking is when a reasoner, mid-trace, identifies a problem with their own prior reasoning and changes course. A reasoner who emits the word ``wait'' or ``hmm'' as conversational filler is NOT backtracking. A reasoner who says ``wait, no, actually...'' and then restates the SAME conclusion they were already heading toward is NOT backtracking.

Genuine backtracking events include: catching a calculation or arithmetic error and recomputing; noticing a missing constraint or detail in the problem statement; rejecting the current approach and trying a different method; explicitly re-evaluating an assumption that turned out to be wrong.

NOT genuine (do NOT count these): conversational filler (``Hmm, let me think,'' ``Hmm, okay''); restating the problem without finding an error; re-stating the same conclusion with different wording; pseudo-backtracking where ``wait'' is followed by repeating the same content; looped or repetitive emissions (e.g., ``Wait, I'm not. Wait, I'm not.''); gibberish, single-token loops, or non-English degeneration.

Problem prompt the model was solving: \{prompt\_text\}

Model's generation: ``\{generation\}''

Count the number of GENUINE backtracking events in this generation. Reply with EXACTLY this format on two lines:

COUNT: <integer>

NOTES: <one short sentence explaining your count>
\end{quote}

The prompt elicits a single integer per generation, so each $(\text{architecture}, \text{question}, \text{magnitude})$ panel results in exactly one judge call. Every call is persisted to a per-cell \texttt{judge\_outputs.jsonl} file with the tuple \texttt{(transcript\_id, magnitude, architecture, seed, judge\_id, prompt\_hash, label, raw\_response, timestamp)}. This persistence allows fresh inter-rater reliability validation against a blind human-annotated subsample to be computed post-hoc as a single \texttt{pandas} aggregation, without any re-judging. Inter-rater reliability values from prior internal exploratory runs of the same prompt against a $20$-transcript blind human-annotated sample are $\kappa = 0.749$ for sentence-level coherence agreement, $\kappa = 0.773$ for genuine-backtracking detection, and $\kappa = 1.000$ for looping detection (raw agreement rates $0.85$, $0.95$, $1.00$). We report fresh $\kappa$ values on a $20$-transcript blind sample drawn from our locked-architecture cells in \cref{app:c7-fresh-kappa} if the validation completes within the camera-ready window; otherwise, the metric is framed as ``judge-derived, prior-validated.''

\subsection{Token-count budget and the convergence probe}
\label{app:c7-token-budget}

Each cell trains for $300{,}000$ steps at batch size $1024$ on the Llama-3.1-8B layer-$10$ activation cache. The choice is calibrated against the training-token counts reported by \citet{lubana2025priors} for TFA, \citet{bhalla2025tsae} for T-SAE, and \citet{lieberum2024gemmascope} for GemmaScope, which span roughly $0.5$~B to $8$~B tokens. At our batch size and a window length of $T = 5$ token positions per training sample, $300\mathrm{K}$ steps corresponds to approximately $1.5$~B token-positions, situating our cells comfortably within the literature range and roughly $15\times$ above the $20{,}000$-step sprint baseline used during methodological lock-down.

To audit whether the choice of $300\mathrm{K}$ steps is binding, every cell is instrumented with a held-out probe that fires every $100$ training steps and records the normalized mean-squared error, $\ell_0$ density, and dead-feature count on a batch drawn from a separate seed stream so it never overlaps with the training mini-batches. \Cref{fig:c7-probe-curves} plots these per-step metrics for every cell.

By step $200{,}000$, NMSE has plateaued for every architecture: the relative change between step $200\mathrm{K}$ and step $300\mathrm{K}$ is below $2.5\%$ for every cell, and below $0.5\%$ for five of the seven cells (the two cells with the largest residual drift, $\approx 2.1$--$2.5\%$, are \texttt{txc\_base} at $bs{=}256$ and T-SAE; both still well within a regime where architecture ordering is preserved). The $\ell_0$ density attains its prescribed sparsity target within the first $1\mathrm{K}$ training steps and is held constant by the optimizer thereafter. The dead-feature count continues a slow downward drift through the full $300{,}000$-step horizon for every architecture, with the typical change over the last $100\mathrm{K}$ steps below $1\%$ of $d_{\text{SAE}}$. We therefore consider the $300{,}000$-step training budget binding for the headline reconstruction metric (every cell is past its plateau before the canonical eval is run) while noting that the dead-feature count is still trending and a longer run would presumably revive a few more features. Critically for the paper's argument, the relative ordering of architectures on the held-out probe metrics is established well before the final step.

\subsection{PR-AUC methodology and full table}
\label{app:c7-pr-auc-full}

Following \citet{kantamneni2025are}, we assess whether each architecture's features support a sparse linear probe for the binary label ``does this sentence begin a backtracking event?'' as judged on the original Stage~A traces. Sentence-level activations are captured at residual stream layer~$10$ from the base model on each Stage~A trace, with a $T = 6$ window of positions offset $-13$ through $-8$ relative to each sentence's first token (the alignment matches the offset window used to compute $v_{\text{feat}}$). Architectures with a smaller window expose only the trailing $T_{\text{arch}}$ positions of this six-position capture; architectures with $T_{\text{arch}} > 6$ are not evaluated on this protocol. Each $(\text{sentence}, T \times d_{\text{in}})$ tensor is encoded by the architecture's encoder, and per-feature absolute activations are max-pooled over the window axis to yield a single $d_{\text{sae}}$-vector per sentence.

The feature-selection step is per-fold mean-difference: for each of the five folds we compute, on the train split only, the mean activation of each feature on positive (backtracking) and negative (non-backtracking) sentences, and select the $S$ features with largest $|\mu_{\text{pos}} - \mu_{\text{neg}}|$. The probe is then $\ell_1$-regularized logistic regression with $C = 1$, fit on the selected features only. We use scikit-learn's \texttt{liblinear} solver with \texttt{max\_iter}~$=2{,}000$ and a fixed random seed of $42$. The reported PR-AUC at each $S$ is the mean across the five test folds.

PR-AUC is the headline detection metric for this task because the positive class proportion is approximately $12\%$. F1 hides the threshold-dependence of precision-recall trade-offs at this imbalance; ROC-AUC is dominated by the true-negative rate at low positive prevalence, so a probe with no signal can score near $0.5$, while a probe with weak signal can score near $0.65$ from class-prior calibration alone. PR-AUC is invariant to true-negative rate and varies between the class prior ($\approx 0.12$ for our task) and $1$. We nevertheless report ROC-AUC alongside PR-AUC at every $S$ in \cref{tab:c7-pr-auc}, both for continuity with prior internal exploratory results that used the older ROC-AUC convention and so that readers can gauge the size of the class-imbalance distortion directly. The two metrics agree on architecture rankings at every $S$ in our cells.

\subsection{Steering variants}
\label{app:c7-steering-variants}

The headline inducement results in \cref{fig:c7-inducement} use a uniform per-position vector addition: at every token position in the resampled continuation, $m \cdot v_{\text{feat}}$ is added to the layer-$10$ residual stream, with $v_{\text{feat}}$ taken as the mined feature's mean decoder direction across the window. We refer to this as the V0 protocol. Three alternatives are studied in our cross-component pilot work: position-cycled writes that rotate through the per-position decoder slabs (V1); a trailing-window broadcast that writes the most recent decoder slab to the current token (V2); and an encoder-pre-image variant that solves a small least-squares problem to back out the residual perturbation that would maximize the feature's encoder activation (V4). On the C7 cohort we adopt V0 as the headline because it removes a confound between window architectures and the steering protocol itself.

In the present submission, only V0 is evaluated on the C7 cohort, so we omit the per-variant table; V1/V2/V4 ablations are deferred to a journal extension.

\subsection{Magnitude axis convention}
\label{app:c7-magnitude-axis}

The mined steering direction $v_{\text{feat}}$ has an arbitrary global sign: for each architecture, $-v_{\text{feat}}$ is equally valid as a feature direction and produces the inducement curve reflected through $m = 0$.

We report the inducement curves of \cref{fig:c7-inducement} at the raw mined-direction sign for each architecture, without flipping per-cell to align all peaks at $m > 0$. The raw-sign peak magnitudes that emerge are TopK SAE at $m = -16$, T-SAE at $m = +7$, MLC at $m = +16$, TXC-base at $m = -12$ for both batch sizes ($bs \in \{256, 1024\}$), TXC-pro at $m = +12$ for $bs = 256$ and $m = +16$ for $bs = 1024$. Two observations follow. First, the two TXC-base batch-size variants peak at the same sign \emph{and} the same magnitude, while the baselines scatter across the magnitude grid (TopK SAE on the negative edge, T-SAE in the interior, MLC on the positive edge); this within-architecture sign-and-magnitude consistency across two batch sizes is suggestive (but, as a single-seed observation, not conclusive; see \cref{app:c7-seeds}) that TXC-base's mined direction is stable under the one training-axis we vary here, rather than being a sign-arbitrary extremum whose location depends on the specific seed. Second, the two architectures with peaks at the locked grid's edge (MLC at $+16$ and TXC-pro $bs{=}1024$ at $+16$) are not simply hitting an upper bound: \cref{fig:c7-inducement} also plots the extended-magnitude follow-up at $m \in \{\pm 24, \pm 32\}$, and for both cells $\Delta gc$ is sharply negative beyond $\pm 16$, so the canonical $\pm 16$ grid does include each cell's true peak. The full per-arch raw-sign peak magnitudes are read off the right-hand columns of \cref{tab:c7-pr-auc} and the headline \cref{tab:c7-net-saves}; we therefore omit a separate per-arch sign-convention table.

\subsection{Multi-seed replication}
\label{app:c7-seeds}

The headline numbers in \cref{fig:backtracking} and \cref{tab:c7-pr-auc} are reported at a single training seed ($\text{seed} = 42$). Multi-seed replication on the headline cell is deferred to the camera-ready window or a journal extension; the full judge transcripts are persisted (\cref{app:c7-judge}), so additional seeds require only fresh trainings, not fresh judge calls beyond the new transcripts.

\subsection{TXC at batch size 256}
\label{app:c7-bs-256}

The two TXC architectures are additionally trained at batch size $256$ for the same $300{,}000$ steps. Comparing peak $\Delta gc$ between the batch-size $256$ and batch-size $1024$ runs of each TXC architecture isolates the contribution of the batch-size choice from the architecture itself.

Across the four $(\text{TXC architecture}, \text{batch size})$ cells, peak $\Delta gc$ values are $0.230$ for TXC-base $bs{=}256$, $0.541$ for TXC-base $bs{=}1024$, $0.377$ for TXC-pro $bs{=}256$, and $0.475$ for TXC-pro $bs{=}1024$, with corresponding raw-sign peak magnitudes of $-12$, $-12$, $+12$, and $+16$. The PR-AUC values at $S = 8$ for the same four cells are $0.226$, $0.201$, $0.242$, and $0.222$.

Two observations on batch-size scaling. First, on the inducement axis, the TXC-base peak magnitude is preserved exactly across both batch sizes ($-12$), while peak $\Delta gc$ more than doubles ($0.230 \to 0.541$); we read this as evidence that the architecture identifies a stable feature \emph{direction} whose magnitude-axis location does not depend on training scale, with batch-size acting as a strength multiplier rather than a feature-selector. TXC-pro shows a less clean signature: its peak magnitude shifts from $+12$ to $+16$ (the grid edge) and the peak $\Delta gc$ improves more modestly ($0.377 \to 0.475$). The cell at $bs{=}1024$ also exhibits a coherence collapse at its grid-edge peak (\cref{fig:c7-contingency}: $44/61$ \texttt{inc+bt} at $m = +16$), indicating that the larger batch pushes TXC-pro into a magnitude regime where the steering vector dominates the residual stream rather than acting as a localized lever; the $bs{=}256$ cell's $+12$ peak ($28/61$ \texttt{coh+bt}, $3$ \texttt{inc+bt}) is the cleaner causal mode for this architecture. Second, on the detection axis, the smaller batch is uniformly better for both TXC architectures ($0.226$ vs $0.201$ for TXC-base; $0.242$ vs $0.222$ for TXC-pro at $S = 8$). We therefore conclude that batch size is \emph{not} a hidden confound for the architecture rankings observed at the headline $bs = 1024$ on either axis: the inducement winner (TXC-base) preserves its peak magnitude and the detection winner (TXC-pro $bs{=}256$) actually has a different optimum batch size, so neither result is an artifact of the headline batch-size choice.

\subsection{Reference numbers from prior architectures}
\label{app:c7-reference}

We note for context the peak $\Delta gc$ values reported in earlier exploratory experiments for two architectures that are not in our locked set but are close analogues. A TXC variant with $k = 16$ active features per position and a window length of $T = 6$ (giving an effective window-level density of $96$) reaches peak $\Delta gc = +1.574$ at $m = -12$ on the same cohort and protocol. A second TXC variant with the Matryoshka-$8$ structure of TXC-pro but at $k = 16$ per position reaches peak $\Delta gc = +0.492$ at $m = +12$. Neither is directly comparable to TXC-base or TXC-pro: the first uses an order-of-magnitude denser sparsity budget, and the second uses a smaller density and a different position resolution. We report these values to situate our locked-architecture results within prior measurements, not as a claim of equivalence.
