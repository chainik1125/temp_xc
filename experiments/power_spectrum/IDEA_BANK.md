## TXC experiment idea bank

**Purpose:** collect plausible TXC use cases without quietly promoting them to
positive evidence. Each entry should state the mechanism, the strongest cheap
baseline, the likely failure mode, and a kill criterion before proposing a
large training sweep.

## Many-shot jailbreaks as in-context persona updating

**Status:** high-interest proposal; raw-information gate not yet run.

### Motivation

The [Persona Selection Model](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/psm/)
gives a particularly clean interpretation of many-shot jailbreaks. In its
“Statement of the persona selection model,” pre-training supplies a
distribution over possible Assistant personas; training episodes update that
distribution as evidence; and runtime context further conditions the
posterior. The authors explicitly interpret
[many-shot jailbreaking](https://www.anthropic.com/research/many-shot-jailbreaking)
as many faux dialogues providing overwhelming evidence that the Assistant
complies with all requests. Empirically, the attack becomes more effective as
the number of demonstrations grows, with the original study testing as many
as 256 shots.

This is a more sharply specified temporal hypothesis than generic persona
drift. We know the evidence items, their order, and their count. We can measure
an internal trajectory after each item and a behavioral endpoint at the final
query. The candidate latent is not merely “unsafe content”; it is something
like the posterior weight on a compliant-Assistant persona, or the accumulated
evidence/log-odds that this dialogue's Assistant complies.

### Why a TXC might help

- The behavior is driven by repeated evidence distributed over a long prompt,
  not by a single explicit instruction.
- Shot count supplies an intervention-like dose axis. A useful feature should
  move smoothly or exhibit a phase transition as shots accumulate, and should
  predict the final compliance transition across held-out targets and
  demonstration templates.
- Mixed evidence schedules can separate a true update trajectory from a bag
  of unsafe tokens. With the number of compliant and refusing examples fixed,
  “compliance then refusal” and “refusal then compliance” test recency,
  hysteresis, and order.
- If the posterior is distributed across several recent shot-boundary states,
  a shared sparse window code could compact it better than an SAE at the final
  boundary.

The last point is the actual TXC hypothesis. The first three only establish a
good temporal benchmark.

### Why it may fail

- Attention may already write the in-context posterior into the current
  residual. If a linear probe on the final shot boundary matches the complete
  history, this is another current-state-sufficiency kill.
- A sequence containing only compliant demonstrations is approximately an
  exchangeable evidence set. Sum/mean pooling or a one-dimensional count may
  be the correct sufficient statistic; a TXC win there would be compression,
  not order-sensitive temporal discovery.
- Token-local windows cannot span tens or hundreds of demonstrations. A
  token-window TXC either sees too little of the attack or becomes infeasible.
- The most predictive feature may be local lexical content, the last
  demonstration's answer style, or an ordinary refusal direction rather than
  a gradually updated persona variable.
- Modern safety mitigations may classify or rewrite the prompt outside the
  open-weight model. The benchmark must use a model and inference stack where
  the measured behavior is generated by the activations being encoded.

### Simplest decisive screen

1. Choose one open-weight, long-context chat model with a measurable
   many-shot compliance curve. Use a published evaluation set or a controlled,
   non-operational safety-policy proxy; store only IDs, scores, and redacted
   text in shared artifacts.
2. Build shot-count ladders such as $N \in \{0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64\}$. Add mixed
   compliant/refusing schedules with matched counts and matched final
   demonstration, plus benign-topic and repeated-example controls.
3. Capture residuals at a fixed boundary after every Assistant demonstration
   and immediately before the target answer, at a small layer grid. Treat one
   dialogue turn as the temporal unit; do not pretend that a five-token window
   reaches a 64-shot process.
4. Predict a continuous endpoint—the target compliance/refusal logit or
   calibrated harmfulness score—from:

   - the current boundary residual;
   - the last $W$ boundary residuals;
   - their mean, max, and exponentially weighted sum;
   - a supervised state-space or cumulative-evidence baseline.

5. Stop before dictionary training unless history beats the current residual
   on held-out target topics and prompt templates. Also require a matched-count
   order or recency effect if the claim is specifically temporal rather than
   set aggregation.
6. If the gate passes, pool token activations within each demonstration and
   train a *shot-level* TXC over $W \in \{4,8,16\}$ pooled turns. Compare at
   matched active-code bandwidth with:

   - final-boundary SAE;
   - SAE latents pooled over the same turns;
   - positional/stacked SAE;
   - DeepSets-style order-invariant pooling;
   - the supervised raw-history ceiling.

7. Mine features on a discovery split only. On held-out dialogues, test
   whether the selected feature tracks the shot-count trajectory, distinguishes
   matched-count schedules, and predicts the behavioral transition.
8. Only after those representation tests, steer the selected feature at the
   final target boundary and measure the shift in the compliance curve. Match
   intervention energy to final-token SAE and pooled-SAE directions and include
   a zero-magnitude no-op.

### Success and kill criteria

**Initial sign of life:** raw shot history improves held-out prediction over
the current boundary; a shot-level TXC retains that gain in a sparse code and
beats pooled SAE/DeepSets at the same active bandwidth; and the feature's
trajectory generalizes across target topics and templates.

**Strong TXC result:** matched-count order schedules produce different latent
trajectories and outcomes, the ordered TXC captures this better than
order-invariant controls, and equal-energy steering causally shifts the
many-shot compliance threshold more than final-token SAE steering.

**Kill:** current-boundary residuals saturate the raw-history ceiling, or a
count/mean-pooled baseline matches the TXC. Either outcome would still support
the Persona Selection Model interpretation of many-shot jailbreaking, but not
the need for a temporal crosscoder.

### Why this is worth screening

Unlike the earlier free-form persona-drift experiment, this task supplies a
known sequence of evidence updates and a behavioral dose-response curve. It
also directly exposes the two questions our graveyard says must be separated:

- Is the accumulated persona evidence already ambient at the current token?
- If not, does the useful history require ordered joint structure, or merely
  pooling/counting?

That makes many-shot jailbreaks a good *falsifiable screen* for the remaining
TXC niche, even though the skeptical prior should be that current-state or
simple evidence pooling will win.
